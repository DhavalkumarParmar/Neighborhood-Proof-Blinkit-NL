# Real numbers from the data layer

Every figure below was produced by querying `data/*.json` and running the real
retrieval code (`lib/proof-core.ts`). Nothing here is estimated. Regenerate with
`npm run check:retrieval`.

Note on provenance: the data is **synthetic**, generated by
`scripts/generate-data.mjs` with a seeded PRNG. These are real counts of real
rows in the repo — they are not real Blinkit orders. Say that on the slide.

These category rates are the **baseline** every product is judged against. They
are computed network-wide, not per store — one store does not have the volume to
make a per-category rate mean anything.

Worth saying out loud on a slide: electronics come back **4× more often than
baby care**. That spread is what makes "that's low for beauty" a real statement
rather than decoration.

## The four states, counted

150 SKUs × 5 stores = **750 SKU-store combinations**. Each resolves to exactly
one state:

| Store | Density | Normal | Widened | No data |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkapuri, Vadodara | high | **140** | 10 | 0 |
| Gotri, Vadodara | low | 100 | **50** | 0 |
| Sector 62, Noida | high | 136 | 14 | 0 |
| Sector 128, Noida | medium | 103 | 47 | 0 |
| Karjat | very low | 6 | 0 | **144** |
| **All 750 combinations** | | **485** | **121** | **144** |

### This is the moat slide

At the dense store, proof works for **140 of 150** products and never once falls
through to "no data". At the thin store, there is nothing honest to say about
**144 of 150** — and the card says so instead of inventing confidence.

A competitor without dark-store density is structurally in the Karjat row.

### Verdict spread across all 750 combinations

| Verdict | Count |
|---|---|
| low (better than category) | 265 |
| normal | 162 |
| high (worse than category) | 179 |

179 combinations show a product in a **worse** light than its category. The
product is willing to say so — that is the credibility argument.
