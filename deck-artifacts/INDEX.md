## The four strongest

**1. `03-anti-hallucination.svg` — your single best slide.**
Every AI demo in the room will claim it is grounded. This one shows the boundary
as a physical divider with the enforcement mechanism named on it. Pair it with
the before/after in artifact 7 and the claim stops being a claim.

**2. The moat table in `05-data-layer-numbers.md`.**

> At the dense store, proof works for 140 of 150 products and never once falls
> through to "no data". At the thin store, there is nothing honest to say about
> 144 of 150 — and the card says so.

That is the defensibility argument as an arithmetic fact rather than an
assertion. A competitor without dark-store density is structurally in the Karjat
row.

**3. `09-worked-example.md`.**
The serum has a 4.2% return rate against a 6.4% category average. It is a good
product, and the card still says the pump breaks. That single example carries
the entire product thesis — honest beats flattering — and it is traceable from
raw rows to rendered pixels.

**4. The before/after in `07-prompt-engineering.md`.**
The model wrote "The return rate was 4." and the guard let it through, because
`4` was a figure we had supplied. Fixing that required code, not a better
prompt. This is the most credible thing in the pack because it is a failure you
found and closed, and it pre-empts the obvious challenge from the room.

## Two things before presenting

**Screenshot these four.** Live and verified — all four states render correctly
in production and the model layer is running there:

```
https://neighborhood-proof-blinkit-nl.vercel.app/p/BEA-0001?p=regular     normal
https://neighborhood-proof-blinkit-nl.vercel.app/p/PET-0010?p=regular     widened to city
https://neighborhood-proof-blinkit-nl.vercel.app/p/BEA-0003?p=regular     honest negative
https://neighborhood-proof-blinkit-nl.vercel.app/p/BEA-0001?p=bachelor    no data
```

Note the first and last are **the same SKU seen by two different shoppers**.
Screenshot them side by side — one product, two pincodes, "212 homes near you"
becomes "nothing to show you". That is the moat in one image and it needs no
explanation.

**Say the data is synthetic, once, early.** All 56,522 rows are generated by
`scripts/generate-data.mjs`. The counts are real counts of real rows; they are
not real Blinkit orders. Getting caught on this would cost more than declaring
it.

## What is not in here, and why

- **No conversion, engagement or revenue numbers.** Nothing in the build
  measures user behaviour. Any such figure would be invented.
- **No competitive benchmarking.** Not in the codebase.
- **No cost-per-call or unit economics.** No billing data available; latency was
  measured (1.4–1.8 s) but spend was not.
- **No accessibility results.** No audit was run.

If the deck needs any of these, they have to come from somewhere other than this
repo.
